People In The Supermarket

Observational essay · 22 June 2026 · Sample

There is a woman in the supermarket holding two tomatoes like they have personally disappointed her. She turns one over, then the other. Behind her, a man sighs into his own chest because the world has asked him to wait twelve seconds.

I watch people in places like this and understand why I prefer distance. Not hatred exactly. Hatred takes too much energy and gives people too much importance. It is more like fatigue. A long, low tiredness with the species.

Still, there is beauty. The old man counting coins in his palm. The child pressing her face against the freezer glass. The checkout girl with blue fingernails moving groceries across the scanner as if she is playing a tired instrument.

Humanity is mostly noise, appetite, vanity, and queueing. But now and then, in the fluorescent ugliness, something small and almost holy slips through. It does not redeem us. Redemption is a salesman’s word. It only proves the light can still find a crack.

The Public Smile

Observational essay · 22 June 2026 · Sample

Everybody has learned the public smile. The mouth opens just enough to prove there is no danger. The eyes stay dead because the eyes are less obedient. You see it at work, in shops, at school gates, in meetings where people say wonderful when they mean nothing.

The modern person has been trained to seem fine. Pain gets ironed flat. Anger becomes professionalism. Loneliness becomes a calendar full of obligations. Even despair has to dress nicely before it is allowed indoors.

I do not trust the cheerful. Not because joy is impossible, but because cheerfulness is often security lighting around an empty building.

And yet sometimes a real smile appears by accident. A tired nurse at a crossing. A mechanic laughing alone at something on his phone. A stranger seeing a dog do something stupid. For one second the mask drops and the animal is visible. Poor thing. Beautiful thing. Doomed thing.

There May Be No Point

Observational essay · 22 June 2026 · Sample

There may be no point to us. This is not as dramatic as people make it. The trees seem to handle it. The rain does not ask for meaning before falling on the roof. A bird will sing on a fence beside a roadkill possum and not consider the contradiction.

People are the ones who need a banner over everything. Purpose. Mission. Legacy. The great human craving to turn breathing into an achievement.

I have seen enough of us to doubt the grand story. We build little kingdoms out of plastic and debt. We mistake attention for love. We hurt each other in ordinary ways and then call it life experience.

But there is the afternoon light on a dirty kitchen wall. There is steam lifting from a cup. There is a woman on the bus resting her head against the window while the city drags itself home. These things do not save the species. They only make the sentence harder to finish.

Morning In The Quiet House

Prose · 22 June 2026 · Sample

The house had the moral temperature of an apology nobody wanted to make. Outside, the bins waited by the kerb like obedient animals. Inside, the kettle clicked off and left the room with nothing but its own thinking.

He stood there in his socks, half alive, considering the great fraud of another day.

The Man At The Counter

Vignette · 22 June 2026 · Sample

He counted his coins slowly, not because he lacked money, but because it was the last piece of control available to him. The girl behind the counter smiled the tired smile of someone paid to forgive the public eight hours at a time.

Behind them both, a screen shouted specials into the air.

The Room Without Pictures

Short story · 22 June 2026 · Sample opening

When Mara moved into the room, she noticed there were no marks on the walls. No nail holes. No faded rectangles where photographs had protected the paint from sunlight. It was a room that had refused memory.

That should have comforted her. Instead, it made her feel expected.

About

About this site

This site is intentionally plain. It is built for reading on old laptops, slow phones, cheap tablets, Kindle browsers, and anything else that can open a basic HTML page.

The work here leans misanthropic, observational, literary, and suspicious of easy answers. It is not motivational writing. It is not content. It is not therapy. It is a record of looking at the world without pretending to enjoy the performance.
